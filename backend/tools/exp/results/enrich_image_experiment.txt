\subsubsection{\textbf{Enrichment Ablation: Image-to-Image vs.\ Image-to-Text Retrieval}}
\label{sec:exp-enrich-image}

\paragraph{Sample Construction.}
We evaluate, under an identical image input, which of two pipelines better exploits
a user-supplied photo. Using a leave-one-out protocol, for each query product $P$ we
treat $P$'s own image as the ``uploaded'' photo and define its relevant (gold) set as
the other products sharing the same \emph{visual category}
(\texttt{visual\_attrs.product\_category}):
\[
  \mathrm{Gold}(P)=\{\,j : \mathrm{cat}(j)=\mathrm{cat}(P)\ \wedge\ j\neq P\,\}.
\]
We sample $N{=}30$ query products (seed $42$) drawn from categories with at least four
members, over a catalog of $1{,}516$ products of which $1{,}493$ carry a $1024$-dim
DashScope \texttt{multimodal-embedding-v1} image vector.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
Both conditions receive the \emph{same} input image and return a top-$K$ ranking
($K{=}30$, query product excluded):
\textbf{A (image-to-image)} encodes the input image into a vector and ranks products
by cosine similarity against their stored image embeddings
(\texttt{image\_embedding} + \texttt{visual\_recall});
\textbf{B (image-to-text)} first uses Qwen-VL to describe the image into English
\texttt{search\_keywords}, then issues those keywords to the production text search
over the product \texttt{name} (\texttt{describe\_shopping\_image} + \texttt{server.search}).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Let $\mathrm{ranked}$ be the ranked list and $G=\mathrm{Gold}(P)$, with binary
relevance $rel_i\in\{0,1\}$. We report three complementary top-$K$ measures:
\[
  \mathrm{precision@}K=\frac{\lvert G\cap \mathrm{ranked}_{1:K}\rvert}{K},\quad
  \mathrm{recall@}K=\frac{\lvert G\cap \mathrm{ranked}_{1:K}\rvert}{\lvert G\rvert},\quad
  \mathrm{nDCG@}K=\frac{\mathrm{DCG@}K}{\mathrm{IDCG@}K},
\]
where $\mathrm{DCG@}K=\sum_{i=1}^{K} rel_i/\log_2(i+1)$. Intuitively,
\emph{precision@}$K$ measures the \emph{purity} of the shortlist (what fraction of the
returned top-$K$ is actually relevant); \emph{recall@}$K$ measures \emph{coverage}
(what fraction of all relevant products the shortlist manages to surface); and
\emph{nDCG@}$K$ measures \emph{ranking quality}, rewarding relevant items placed near
the top and discounting those found lower down, normalised to $[0,1]$ against the ideal
ordering. Precision and recall trade off as $K$ grows---a longer list covers more but
is less pure---so they are read together, while nDCG summarises whether the relevant
items that are found are also well ordered. All metrics are averaged over the $N$
queries.

To test whether the A--B gap is systematic rather than driven by a few lucky queries,
we use a per-query paired \emph{Wilcoxon signed-rank test} on the metric values. It is
a non-parametric paired test (no normality assumption): it ranks the absolute per-query
differences $\mathrm{metric}_{\mathrm{A}}-\mathrm{metric}_{\mathrm{B}}$ and checks
whether positive differences dominate. A small $p$-value therefore indicates that A's
advantage holds consistently across the query set; we mark ${}^{*}$, ${}^{**}$,
${}^{***}$ at the $0.05$, $0.01$, $0.001$ thresholds. $\Delta=\mathrm{metric}_{\mathrm{A}}-\mathrm{metric}_{\mathrm{B}}$.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:exp-enrich-image} shows that image-to-image (A) dominates image-to-text
(B) on all three metrics, and every difference is statistically significant under the
paired Wilcoxon test ($p<0.01$). The pattern is consistent: A is not only more precise
at the top of the list, but also recovers a substantially larger share of the relevant
catalogue (its recall gain is the largest) and orders those hits better (higher nDCG).
In other words, when a photo is available, matching directly in the visual embedding
space surfaces more of the right products and ranks them higher than first verbalising
the image into keywords. The gap stems from the double lossy conversion in image-to-text
(\emph{vision $\rightarrow$ text $\rightarrow$ text matching}): the intermediate keywords
are prone to cross-category drift---e.g.\ a bra photo described as ``lace / lingerie''
retrieves wigs and hair extensions whose titles share those words---whereas
image-to-image compares directly in the visual space and reliably stays within the
correct category. This confirms the value of the offline \texttt{image\_embedding}
enrichment as the primary retrieval path when a photo is provided.
